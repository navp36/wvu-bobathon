Judge scorecard · WVU Bobathon

Score a submission

Fill one scorecard per team. Ask the questions, rate each of the five criteria from 1 to 10, and explain your reasoning in the notes. The total calculates itself.

Your entries stay on this device. Nothing is sent anywhere. They are kept in this browser tab only so a refresh won't lose them; closing the tab clears them. Before you move on, use Copy summary or Print at the bottom to keep a record. Refer to teams by project or team name; don't enter student contact details.

2 · Questions to ask

Let the team tell the story

You don't need to ask every question. Tick the ones you asked and jot down anything that stands out; those notes will help when you score.

  1. 1
    What inspired your idea/project?
  2. 2
    How did you decide on your approach?
  3. 3
    How did you make sure your solution is usable for the target audience?
  4. 4
    What did you learn during your project (technical/nontechnical)?
  5. 5
    What was a challenge you faced working on this project?
  6. 6
    How did your team handle areas where no one had prior experience?
  7. 7
    How did you divide the work among your team?
  8. 8
    What makes your project different from competitors or other existing solutions?
  9. 9
    If you had more time, what features would you add/improve?

3 · Scoring

Rate each criterion from 1 to 10

Drag a slider (or focus it and use the arrow keys). A criterion counts toward the total only after you've rated it, so an untouched slider never adds points by accident. Use the notes box to explain the score.

Suggested scale (agree on it with the other judges first)
ScoreMeaning
1–2Little or no evidence
3–4Below expectations
5–6Meets basic expectations
7–8Strong
9–10Outstanding

Scores are only comparable if every judge uses the scale the same way. Agree on it before the first demo, and score each team on its own merits rather than against the team before it.
